The main rule: change only what you asked for

Seedance doesn't know what matters to you. If you don't tell it what to keep, it may change the face, the movement and the camera too. That's why every prompt has two parts.

CHANGE

Whatever you asked for:

  • outfit
  • location & background
  • lighting
  • scene details
  • camera angle — only on request

KEEP

Everything else:

  • face
  • hair
  • body & proportions
  • hands
  • movement & gestures
  • facial expressions
  • composition
  • camera
  • timing

By default, the camera stays as it was. Want a different angle? Say so in the prompt.

How to do it: 3 steps

  1. 01

    Get your video and reference photo ready

    Pick a video and find a photo of what you want to get.

    You don't have to find a reference — you can generate one.

    Take a screenshot of a frame from your video and ask ChatGPT Images or Nano Banana to change the outfit, the background, or both. Use the resulting image as your reference.

    A reference like this works especially well: the pose, angle and composition already match your video. If the face on the generated image comes out a little different, that's fine — Seedance takes the face from your original video anyway.

    The person in the reference shouldn't replace yours. The face, hair and body should come from your original video, and only the outfit or the location from the photo. You have to say this explicitly in the prompt — the template below and the Prompt Engine already do.

    Using more than one reference? Decide in advance which photo does what. For example: the first one is the outfit, the second one is the location. One photo can cover both the outfit and the location, but then you have to say so explicitly in the prompt.

  2. 02

    Write the prompt — pick an option

    Option A Yourself, with a simple template

    Copy the template and replace the text in [square brackets]. Write the prompt in English — it's more reliable.

    @Video1 is the original video. Keep the person exactly the same: face, hair, body, hands, movements, facial expressions and timing. @Image1 is the reference for [the outfit / the location / the outfit and the location]. Do not copy the person from @Image1. Change only: [her dress to the dress from @Image1]. Details: [dark blue fitted mini dress, sleeveless, high neckline]. Keep everything else unchanged. Keep the camera angle and camera movement the same as in @Video1. Match the light on the person to the new scene. Realistic result, no distortions.
    A template for simple edits: one item or one location.
    What's what
    • @Video1 and @Image1 — this is how Seedance knows which file you mean. @Video1 is your video, @Image1 is the first photo you upload, @Image2 the second.
    • [the outfit / the location / the outfit and the location] — pick what you're changing: the outfit, the location, or both.
    • Change only — what to change, in one sentence. For example: Change only: her dress and the location — use both from @Image1.
    • Details — be specific: color, fabric, cut, or the place itself. The AI doesn't treat words like “beautiful” or “cinematic” as instructions.
    • Leave the other lines as they are: they protect the person and the camera.

    Two references? Replace the second line with these two:

    @Image1 is the reference for the outfit only. @Image2 is the reference for the location only.

Common mistakes

Writing “make it look like the photo”

The model won't know what to take. Give it a role: @Image1 is the reference for the outfit.

Not saying what to keep

Then Seedance may change the face, the movement and the camera.

Describing things in general terms

“Beautiful” isn't an instruction. Describe the color, the fabric, the place, the light.

Uploading the Prompt Engine or the screenshot to Seedance

They're only for ChatGPT or Claude.

Why a simple template isn't always enough

The template above gets you a usable result for simple edits. But in a complex scene — say, two people, a new location and moving water — the result depends on details that are easy to miss.

Writing it yourself

You have to think through:

  • the outfit: fabric, cut, color, texture;
  • the new place: what's in the foreground, middle ground and background;
  • light on the person and on the background, so they match;
  • how the fabric moves and reacts to wind;
  • motion in the scene: water, leaves, birds;
  • what to rule out so distortions don't appear.
